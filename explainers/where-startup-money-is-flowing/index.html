Where startup money is flowing: a sector map

A classification framework for reading rounds across AI infra, AI apps, fintech, climate, defense and space, health, SaaS, consumer, and deep tech.

By Komal Kedarnath G 2026-11-05 3 min read

Draft status: Framework note only; intentionally omits invented market totals or unverified figures.

Tracking where startup capital flows is easier when rounds are grouped by what buyers actually pay for rather than broad buzzwords. Dry Powder Dispatch uses nine sector tags alongside six regional buckets so cross-border patterns stay legible.

A sector map is a sorting lens, not a forecast: it shows which problems attract capital and where bottlenecks sit.

Compute, software, and workflow layers

On the software and compute side, we separate AI infra (serving, routing, observability, and underlying tooling) from AI apps (end-user or vertical workflows) and traditional SaaS or consumer products. Distinguishing infrastructure from application layers helps clarify whether capital is funding picks-and-shovels capacity or customer-facing adoption.

Financial rails and physical frontier

Fintech spans payments, credit, treasury, and compliance software across domestic and cross-border corridors. Alongside it sit capital-intensive or hardware-linked categories: climate, defense and space, health, and deep tech. These categories often combine longer engineering cycles with grant, project, or enterprise procurement milestones.

When reviewing rounds across India, the US, Europe, the Middle East, Southeast Asia, and other regions, pair the sector tag with the stage and lead investor to see whether capital is testing early product-market fit or scaling proven distribution.
