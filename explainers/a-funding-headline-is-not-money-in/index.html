A funding headline is not money in the bank

Five questions to ask before you repeat the number.

By Komal Kedarnath G 2026-11-27 1 min read Read on Substack

In February 2026, OpenAI announced $110B in new investment. Amazon's share was $50B, but only $15B came up front. The other $35B was to follow when certain conditions were met.

They were met: Amazon's filing at the end of July showed the rest paid in two stages. Still, on headline day most of that cheque was a promise with strings. Smaller rounds can work the same way.

1. Is it signed money or an intention?

"MOU", "up to" and "intends to" are not cash.

2. All now, or in tranches?

Hypothetical: a $10M round paid as $4M now and $6M on targets is $4M of runway until the targets are hit.

3. Cash, or credits and services?

Credits don't pay salaries. The Register reported that Amazon's and Nvidia's money came tied to large purchase commitments by OpenAI and partners.

4. Equity or debt?

Debt has to be paid back.

5. Does it reach the company?

In a secondary sale or tender offer, the money goes to the people selling shares.

Try it

Take the last funding announcement you read and answer all five. Where the answer isn't public, write "not disclosed".
