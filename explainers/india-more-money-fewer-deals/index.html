India: more money, fewer deals, fewer investors

Indian tech startups raised $2.2 billion in Q3, up 5%. Deal count fell 13% and active investors fell 24%.

Dry Powder Dispatch 2026-11-25 4 min read

Indian tech startups raised $2.2 billion between July and September, up 5% from $2.1 billion a year earlier, according to Inc42's Q3 report. Deal count fell 13% to 210. Active unique investors fell 24% to 511. The median ticket stayed at $3 million.

Four rounds of $100 million or more closed in the quarter, against one in Q3 2025:

  • Emergent, $130 million from Creaegis, Khosla Ventures, SoftBank, Lightspeed and Y Combinator, which made it a unicorn
  • River, $120 million, an electric-vehicle maker
  • Navi, $100 million from Prosus, its first institutional round
  • Pixxel, $100 million from Temasek and others, in spacetech

Where the money went

AI raised $438 million across 35 deals, up 265%. Cleantech raised $433 million, more than three times the year-ago figure, with EV startups at 57% of its deals. Deeptech raised $290 million, up 176%. Fintech raised $249 million, down 11%. E-commerce raised $245 million, down 31%, though it had the most deals at 44.

Bengaluru took $1.4 billion across 80 deals, 64% of the total. Delhi NCR fell 27% to $301 million, and Mumbai fell 64% to $223 million.

Tracxn's nine-month count, reported by MediaBrief, shows $10.3 billion raised by 21 September, up 7%, with funding rounds down 38% to 1,134. Seed funding fell 37% to $698 million, and Bengaluru held 43% of tech capital. Tracxn and Inc42 use different methods, so their totals differ.

Exits and doubts

Inc42 counted 22 M&A deals in the quarter, up from 13, including upGrad's $200 million purchase of Unacademy. Six new-age tech companies listed. Among 85-plus institutional investors it surveyed, 63% said Indian AI valuations had risen beyond comfortable levels.

Mid-November deals

StartupTalky's dailies for 16 and 17 November list nine rounds. Dreamteam raised $53 million for an AI-first CRM, with Together Fund, Accel and Premji Invest. Its founders come from Freshworks. Bharat Semi raised a $24 million seed for AI sensors, led by Info Edge, South Park Commons and Indus Bridge Ventures. The others were small: Kutlerri.ai at $4 million, JohnAIC at $1.9 million, Morsa at $1.5 million and Lemonpeel at $1 million, plus three rupee-denominated rounds.

StartupTalky doesn't give exact dates for these rounds, so the deals page shows "by 16 November" and "around 17 November".

What it means

Fewer investors, bigger cheques. That favours founders who already have a lead and a story. Early-stage funding fell 18% to $164 million across 93 deals, and the median early ticket rose 20% to $1.2 million. If you're raising pre-seed in Bengaluru right now, you're pitching a thinner field of buyers with the same story.
