Funds closed this month: Norwest raised $4 billion and a lot of small funds closed beside it

Two weeks of fund announcements show a barbell: a few very large vehicles and many solo and specialist funds under $100 million.

Dry Powder Dispatch 2026-11-25 4 min read Read on Substack

Norwest announced Norwest 18 on 12 November. The $4 billion fund is the largest in its 65-year history, according to Private Equity List. It targets venture and growth investments in technology, healthcare and consumer companies in the US, India and Israel, and the newsletter says the firm plans $250 million to $350 million a year for India.

That fund sat inside a busy fortnight. Private Equity List counted 26 new funds worth $10.6 billion in the week of 9 to 15 November, and 19 funds worth $27.2 billion the week before. The second week includes buyout, credit and infrastructure vehicles, such as TPG Rise Climate II at $10.4 billion, so it overstates venture.

The larger venture closes

  • Creandum VIII, $750 million, for pre-seed to Series A across Europe. The newsletter says the firm could have raised more.

  • Valor Equity Partners, reportedly about $3.3 billion, for Series A to late stage in aerospace, AI and energy.

  • Yosemite, second fund at $350 million, for oncology.

  • Vine Ventures Fund III, $236 million against a target near $200 million. About half goes to Israeli companies.

  • redalpine VIII, $250 million, closed on 10 November. HackerNoon’s write-up says it is 25% larger than the $200 million predecessor, which the article labels as its own arithmetic. Limited partners include Aviva Investors, Flexstone Partners and KfW.

The small and specialist funds

  • Zacua Ventures Fund II, $135 million, with Volvo Group Venture Capital, Hilti and Holcim among 30-plus corporate and institutional LPs.

  • Course Corrected Fund II, €80 million, backed by more than €34.6 million from the European Investment Fund.

  • Stage 2 Capital, $100 million across two funds, for B2B AI at seed and Series A.

  • Flashpoint Venture Growth Fund IV, $116 million, for vertical AI from European and Israeli founders scaling into the US.

  • Touchstone Partners and Ho Chi Minh City’s state-backed venture fund, a blended-finance vehicle of up to $100 million.

  • Enza Capital’s African AI Fund, $21 million, with first cheques of $250,000 to $5 million.

Some announcements are targets, not closes. Altimeter’s Premier Growth IX shows a $2.5 billion target in an SEC filing and nothing raised yet.

What the pattern says

Our read, and it’s only a read: corporate LPs and public bodies fill many of the small funds. Volvo, Hilti, Holcim, the European Investment Fund and the British Business Bank all appear in this fortnight’s lists. Large funds keep taking larger cheques for later-stage AI, defence and healthcare. The middle thins out. That’s the part founders feel.

For founders, that means a seed round in 2027 will often come from a small specialist fund with a corporate or public LP behind it. Ask who the LPs are. We’d want to know before signing, because a corporate LP may bring distribution and may also bring conflict.
