How money crosses borders in 2026: UPI links, stablecoin rules and the cost of a remittance

The US finalised its stablecoin rule on 21 October. UPI now runs in nine countries. Both are growing faster than the rules that connect them.

Dry Powder Dispatch 2026-11-25 5 min read

Money moves between countries on three kinds of rail. Banks and card networks carry most of it. Linked national payment systems, such as India's UPI, carry a growing slice. Stablecoins, tokens that track a currency, make up the newest slice. Each rail has a rule book, and the rule books don't match.

UPI goes abroad

Business Today lists UPI in nine countries: Bhutan (2021), Singapore (2023), France, Mauritius, Nepal, Sri Lanka and the UAE (2024), Qatar (2025) and Cambodia (2026). The story's headline and body say nine, though its web address says twelve, so check the current list with NPCI International.

The domestic base is large. The same article gives 18,586.60 crore UPI transactions in FY25, up from 13,112.95 crore in FY24 and 4,595.61 crore in FY22. The RBI's annual report, summarised by Glenbrook Partners, says the RBI and NPCI International will work toward taking UPI to 20 countries by FY29.

The US finalises its stablecoin rule

On 21 October the OCC announced its final rule implementing the GENIUS Act. It sets requirements for permitted payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers under OCC supervision, and for some custody activity at OCC-supervised entities. The rule and the Act take effect on 18 January 2027.

Anti-money-laundering and sanctions rules aren't in it. The OCC says those will come in a separate rulemaking with the Treasury Department. It will announce separately when it starts to accept applications and waiver requests.

Rule books don't match

Jai Massari, writing for the Atlantic Council on 24 August, argues that stablecoin regulation has moved faster than international coordination. Under the GENIUS Act, a foreign issuer can qualify if the Treasury judges its home regime comparable. The EU's MiCA has no equivalent route, and it bars offering stablecoins from non-EU issuers in the EU. Both regimes require some reserves to sit locally. An issuer that wants to serve both markets needs separate licensed entities in each.

What a remittance costs

The World Bank's Remittance Prices Worldwide report for the first quarter of 2025 put the global average cost of sending $200 at 6.49%, up from 6.26% in the last quarter of 2024. Sub-Saharan Africa remained the most expensive region to receive money. we couldn't find a newer global average. The World Bank publishes the current series on its site.

Capital flows go the other way too

Money for startups also crosses borders in the opposite direction. The $26 billion private placement beside Anthropic's IPO (see our IPO piece) came from US chip and cloud companies. Gulf sovereign funds backed AI companies in the US, per Global SWF data. Norwest's new $4 billion fund targets the US, India and Israel. Capital for a founder in Bengaluru, Riyadh or London often has a Delaware vehicle at one end and a rupee or riyal bank account at the other.

Analysis: for founders

This section is opinion.

If you sell into India, UPI acceptance in a new country matters more to your customer than to you. Watch for settlement terms before you plan around it.

If you accept stablecoin payments, 18 January 2027 and the Treasury's AML rule decide how formal your counterparties have to be. Until the AML rule is final, ask any issuer how it plans to comply.

If you pay contractors abroad, measure your actual cost per $200 and per $2,000 transfer. The World Bank average describes a typical small remittance, and business payments run on different pricing.
