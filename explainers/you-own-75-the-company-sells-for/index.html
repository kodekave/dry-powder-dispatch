You own 75%. The company sells for $30M. You could get nothing.

Liquidation preferences, worked through with simple numbers.

By Komal Kedarnath G 2026-11-26 2 min read Read on Substack

Founders watch the valuation. The line that decides who gets paid is often a different one: the liquidation preference. It is how much preferred holders, usually investors, get back before common holders, usually founders and team.

The setup

Investors put in $20M for 25% of the company. Founders and team hold 75%. The company sells for $30M. One class of investors, no debt, no fees.

1× non-participating

The investor picks the better of $20M back or 25% of the sale ($7.5M). They take $20M. Everyone else shares $10M. That is a third of the sale for 75% of the shares.

1× participating

The investor takes $20M first, then 25% of what is left: $22.5M in total. Everyone else gets $7.5M.

2× non-participating

The investor is owed $40M. The sale is $30M. They take all of it. Everyone else gets $0.

At a big exit

Sell for $200M instead. With either non-participating version, the investor converts to 25% ($50M) and you get $150M. With 1× participating, the investor gets $65M and you get $135M. With 1× non-participating, converting only beats the preference above $80M.

Why check now

On Carta, more than 10% of rounds in Q3 2026 had participating preferred, the highest in six years, while the share of down rounds fell to 13% (SaaStr's summary of the Carta data).

Before you sign

  • Is the preference 1× or higher?

  • Is it participating? If so, is there a cap?

  • Does a new round sit senior to earlier ones, or alongside them?

  • What does each person get if you sell at the post-money valuation, at half of it, and at a quarter?

If you hold options, ask how the preferences affect you.
