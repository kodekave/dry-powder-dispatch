Briefing

Anthropic priced above its range. Most big 2026 listings still trade below issue.

The deal raised $81.34 billion on 18 November. Of the 53 US IPOs of $1 billion or more this year, 37 sat under their issue price at the 19 November close.

Dry Powder Dispatch 2026-11-25 4 min read Read on Substack

Disclosure: Anthropic makes Claude, which this site uses for research and drafting. Each figure below links to its source.

Anthropic priced its IPO on 18 November at $83 a share. The range set on 10 November was $70 to $78. The company sold 980 million shares and raised $81.34 billion, which Renaissance Capital lists as the largest IPO ever. SpaceX held the record at $75 billion after its June listing.

The terms moved fast. On 10 November, Renaissance reported a $61 billion deal of 825 million shares, with a $1.2 trillion fully diluted valuation at the midpoint. That figure leaves out $111 billion of convertible debt. A $26 billion private placement at the offer price ran alongside the deal, with AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC among the named buyers. Revenue for the twelve months to 30 September was $35.3 billion.

Trading opened on Nasdaq as ANT on 19 November. Stock Titan has the first close at $97.17, up 17.1%, and values the company at about $1.10 trillion at the IPO price. Its count uses shares outstanding after the deal, so it differs from Renaissance’s fully diluted number. Renaissance’s headline for 20 November describes a seesaw in the debut, and its profile page later showed a quote of $79.69 with no date stamp. Check a live quote before you cite the stock. We’d rather you do that than trust our snapshot.

The window is open for a few names

Stock Titan counts 53 US IPOs of $1 billion or more between 1 January and 19 November. They raised $195.6 billion, and Anthropic and SpaceX supplied about 80% of it. At the 19 November close, 37 of the 53 traded below their IPO price. The median sat 21.8% under. Take out the two giants and the other 51 are worth about 25% less than at issue.

The pipeline behind them looks thin. Dealroom reports that Sam Altman told Fortune on 12 September that OpenAI would list in 2027. Kraken is reported for the second quarter of 2027 at the earliest. Oura postponed a $2.2 billion IPO on 29 September and set no new date. Foley, quoting the PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor, counts only 18 US companies going public in the third quarter.

What to take from it

This section is analysis. The reporting is above. Our read: the deal proves the window is open for a company that size. It tells you nothing about yours.

A record deal doesn’t reopen the door for a Series B company. Founders should plan liquidity around tenders and secondaries, and our piece on exits without an IPO covers how those work.

Investors should ask which valuation a mark uses. Renaissance left $111 billion of convertible debt out of its headline value, and Stock Titan counts shares differently again. Public comparables now exist for private AI labs, and the spread between the three numbers is large.

Anthropic planned to sell part of the deal to retail investors through Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi and E*TRADE. The pages I could read give no final size, and the lock-up date sits behind Renaissance’s paywall. Read the prospectus before you model share supply. We haven’t, and neither should you rely on this page for it.
