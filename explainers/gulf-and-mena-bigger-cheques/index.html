MENA startups raised $1.65 billion in Q3. Saudi Arabia led, and fewer companies took the money.

Funding doubled on Q2 and fell 63% on Q3 2025. Nine-month deal count is down a third.

By Komal Kedarnath G 2026-11-25 4 min read Read on Substack

Startups in the Middle East and North Africa raised $1.65 billion across 145 deals in the third quarter, according to Wamda, which compiled the data with Digital Digest. That’s 108% above Q2 and about 63% below the $4.5 billion of Q3 2025. September alone passed $1.1 billion.

Saudi Arabia took the lead at about $1 billion across 62 deals, more than ten times its Q2 figure. The UAE came second at $507 million across 41 deals, down 14%. Egypt followed with $56.4 million across 15 deals.

Fintech took $1.03 billion, about 62% of the total. Debt was $441 million, 26.8% of funding, against almost 59% in 2025.

The largest rounds

Wamda lists these, without naming investors:

  • Barq, $329.5 million Series A at a $1.85 billion valuation

  • Moove, $250 million Series C, in the UAE

  • Tabby, $233 million Series F at a $6.5 billion valuation

  • Lendo, $200 million of debt

Wamda notes that without Moove and Fasset the UAE total would have been $189 million. Without Paymob, Egypt would have been $21.4 million. A few companies set each country’s number, and you shouldn’t read a regional average as a typical round.

The nine-month picture

MAGNiTT’s Q3 update says nine-month funding fell 7% on the year and deal activity fell 33%. Early-stage deals were 82% of activity, the lowest share in a decade. The UAE and Saudi Arabia held 89% of regional funding. MENA M&A fell 26%. MAGNiTT cites the regional conflict and a growing pipeline risk for Q4 and 2027.

Semafor, citing MAGNiTT, puts the nine-month total at $2.98 billion and says the two countries were behind every round of $100 million or more. Wamda’s nine-month figure is about $3.4 billion. The two trackers use different datasets.

Where the capital comes from

Sovereign funds sit behind much of the larger money. Global SWF data reported by Gulf News shows Mubadala investing $12.9 billion in AI and digitalisation in 2025, the Kuwait Investment Authority $6 billion and the Qatar Investment Authority $4 billion. Those figures cover global investment, not only Gulf startups.

What to watch

You can’t price a MENA round off a regional average. With early-stage deals thinning, fewer companies will be ready for growth rounds in 2027. Semafor makes the same point and adds that Gulf money fills the gap left by outside investors who want clear exit routes. M&A fell 26%, so those routes aren’t widening.
