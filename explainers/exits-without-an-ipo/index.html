Exits without an IPO: tenders, secondaries and the funds that sell

Record secondary volume and busy tender calendars carry most of the liquidity load while the IPO window serves a handful of names.

Dry Powder Dispatch 2026-11-25 4 min read

The IPO door reaches few companies. Anthropic and SpaceX supplied about 80% of the money raised by US IPOs of $1 billion or more this year (see our IPO piece). Everyone else needs another door.

Tender offers

A tender lets employees and early holders sell some shares to new or existing investors while the company stays private. Mercor's $750 million Series D at a $20 billion valuation included an employee tender. The company's announcement doesn't say how much of the $750 million went to sellers.

Carta says it administered 71 tender offers worth about $3 billion in the first half of 2026, its highest first half in at least four years. Carta counts only the tenders run on its own platform, so the figure understates the market.

Secondaries

Secondary markets trade existing fund and company stakes. The numbers differ by what each firm counts:

  • Evercore counts $121 billion of private-asset secondaries in the first half of 2026, a record, as managers hold single trophy assets longer.
  • William Blair put 2025 at $220 billion, with single-asset continuation funds growing from $34 billion in 2024 to $60 billion.
  • PitchBook estimated US venture secondaries at $106.3 billion in 2025, including $14.6 billion of GP-led deals.

Don't add these together. Each covers a different slice.

Why the volume is high

PitchBook's Q3 2026 research note pairs record exit value with historically low fund distributions and a growing IPO backlog. Funds hold paper gains and LPs wait for cash. A secondary turns paper into cash without a listing.

India shows the same pull. Inc42 surveyed more than 85 institutional investors, and 38% expect secondary buyouts to give the most liquidity. In mid-November, StartupTalky reported SoftBank selling a ₹1,885 crore Meesho stake. In Europe, Acurio Ventures launched a €115 million fund to buy into the VC secondary market.

Analysis: for founders

This section is opinion. Our read: the tender is the exit most founders will meet first, so learn its terms before you need one.

If a lead offers a tender inside your round, ask who can sell, at what price and how much of the round it uses. Round dollars spent on a tender buy no runway.

Ask whether sellers need to hold shares for a minimum period, and whether you want the same price for everyone. A tender at a discount to the last round resets how your team values its options.

If a fund wants to sell your shares on the secondary market, read your investor rights agreement first. Transfer limits and rights of first refusal usually decide who can buy.
