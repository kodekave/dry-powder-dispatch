The Fed hiked in September and held in October, with two votes to hike again.

The target range sits at 3.75% to 4%. The next meeting runs 8 and 9 December.

By Komal Kedarnath G 2026-11-25 3 min read Read on Substack

The Federal Reserve raised its target range by a quarter point on 16 September, to 3.75% to 4%, in a 12 to 0 vote. On 28 October it held the range. The vote was 10 to 2. Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack voted against the hold. Both wanted another quarter-point increase.

The October statement says economic activity is expanding at a strong pace and inflation remains elevated. It says uncertainty stays elevated in part because of geopolitical developments. It also says more information will help the Committee set the stance it needs for a timely return to 2% inflation.

The next meeting runs 8 and 9 December. It comes with a new set of economic projections.

What venture has shown so far

Carta’s Q3 2026 State of Private Markets report puts down rounds at 13% of priced rounds on its platform, the lowest rate in four years. Companies on Carta raised $49.3 billion across 1,179 rounds in the quarter, and deal count fell 21% on the year. Carta’s page blocked our automated fetch, so we took these numbers from its public listing. Check the full report before you reuse them.

On that data, the September hike hasn’t pushed prices down. It hasn’t brought back volume either.

Analysis: what to do with it

This section is opinion. The facts sit above.

Two dissents toward a hike mean December is a live meeting. Don’t assume a hold. Model your runway at 24 months with no rate cut in it.

Check how your venture debt or equipment line prices. Many facilities float against a policy rate, and a quarter point on a large facility shows up in your burn.

Expect investors to price a policy rate near 4% into their return hurdles. A fund that can earn close to 4% in cash asks more of a seed cheque than it did at 2%.

The IPO route stays narrow (see Anthropic’s IPO), so a rate path that delays exits also delays the cash that funds return to LPs.
