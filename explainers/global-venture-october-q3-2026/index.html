October venture hit $59 billion. Seven billion-dollar rounds took over 40% of it.

Q3 had a record 27 rounds of $1 billion or more. The dollar totals keep rising and the list of companies behind them keeps shrinking.

Dry Powder Dispatch 2026-11-25 4 min read Read on Substack

Global venture funding reached about $59 billion in October, up 45% on a year earlier, according to Crunchbase News data as written up by Value Add VC. Seven rounds of $1 billion or more took just over 40% of the month. AI companies took 66%.

DeepSeek led with $15.9 billion at a $90 billion valuation. The US took $28 billion and China took $20.6 billion. Value Add notes that China passed $20 billion in a single month for the first time since Ant Group’s $14 billion raise in June 2018. Strip out DeepSeek and October comes to about $43 billion. That’s Value Add’s own arithmetic, not a Crunchbase number.

The rest of the top seven:

  • Re:Build, $2.1 billion, led by General Catalyst and JPMorgan Chase

  • Axiom, roughly $2 billion, for data-centre power and grid capacity

  • Ramp, $1.85 billion

  • Tenstorrent, $1.5 billion Series E, for AI chips

  • Kalshi, $1 billion Series E

  • Flapping Airplanes, $1 billion Series A at about a $6 billion valuation

Exits ran hot too. At least nine venture-backed companies sold for more than $1 billion in October, including Uber’s agreed purchase of ezCater.

Q3 told the same story at a larger scale

Crunchbase put Q3 at $159 billion across about 6,000 startups, per AI Weekly and Foley & Lardner. That’s up 53% on Q3 2025 and 25% below Q2. A record 27 rounds crossed $1 billion, against 16 in Q2 and 14 in Q1. Foley says those 27 companies took roughly one third of global capital. Eight raised $3 billion or more, including Databricks and Safe Superintelligence at $5 billion each.

US companies took $91 billion, 57% of the total. Through three quarters, venture funding reached $679 billion, the highest on record for that stretch.

How to read the totals

Read the deal count beside the dollar total. In the data Carta tracks, Q3 deal count fell 21% on the year while dollars stayed high (see our Fed piece). Money is moving into fewer rounds.

Providers disagree. Crunchbase left out Cohere’s reported $5 billion target in October because the company announced no close. Europe’s total changes by 10% or more depending on who counts (see Europe). Quote the provider with every number. You’ll save yourself an argument later.
